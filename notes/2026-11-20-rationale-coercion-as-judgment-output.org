* Summary

=sec:log-rel-lean= offers two renderings of the coercion judgment: put the
derivation in =Type= and define =coerce= by recursion on it, or keep the
relation a =Prop= and prove agreement with =Value.reinterpret=. The section
motivates the choice with "lean can't eliminate a =Prop= into a coercion."

That sentence is true but it doesn't support the dichotomy, because it rules
out the wrong thing. Lean forbids *eliminating* a =Prop= to produce data. It
does not forbid a =Prop=-valued family from being *indexed* by data. A
judgment written

\[ \judg{\Gamma}{\dc_1 \dcompat \dc_2 \coerce \co} \]

can be rendered as an inductive =Prop= whose last argument is the coercion,
built up rule by rule exactly as the report writes it. Reading $\co$ off a
derivation then needs no elimination at all: it is already a component of the
hypothesis's type. So there is a third option, and it is the one that matches
the report's notation most literally.

Two further results, worked out below.

1. *Value finiteness does more than justify termination.* For a recursive
   descriptor pair, finiteness is what makes the coercion *unique*, and so what
   stops the recursive rule from being a soundness hole. It does not, however,
   do anything about the =Prop=/=Type= barrier. The two obstacles are
   independent, and conflating them is what makes the dichotomy look forced.
2. *The judgment can carry a coercion /recipe/ rather than the coercion.* A
   first-order syntax $\Coe$ with a denotation $\interp{\cdot}$ keeps every
   advantage of option (c), makes $\mathsf{fix}$ a constructor instead of a
   semantic fixpoint, and — the decisive point — is what makes a compatibility
   *checker* writable as an ordinary Lean function. This is the recommendation.

This note is the successor to the =Lean= section of
=2026-09-30-review-coercion-framing.org=, whose options (a) and (b) are the two
now in =sec:log-rel-lean=. It also settles the choice E5 (second bullet, in
=2026-09-01-review-proto-relations.md=) asks the author to make, and which R4
of =2026-10-28-finding-descriptor-recursion-symbol-table.org= leaves open: in
favour of the environment route, with the environment finite and first-order.

* 1 What the =Prop= restriction does and does not forbid

The restriction is large elimination: from =h : P= with =P : Prop= you cannot
compute a =Value → Value=, because proofs are irrelevant and two proofs of =P=
must be interchangeable. What the restriction never touched is the *arguments*
of a =Prop=-valued family. These may live in any sort. The frozen InterParse
relation already exercises this — =DescCompat : Desc → Desc → Prop=
(=Compatible.lean:116=) is indexed by two pieces of =Type=-valued data, and
every lemma about it reads those off its hypotheses freely. Adding a third
index of type =Value → Value= is the same move.

The consequence is that "the derivation builds the coercion" and "the coercion
is recoverable in Lean" come apart from each other:

- Can a rule's conclusion name a coercion built from its premises' coercions?
  *Yes* — it is an index, not a computation.
- Given a derivation =h : DescCompat Σ Γ d₁ d₂ c=, is =c= available? *Yes*,
  and runnable: it sits in the type of =h=, so nothing is eliminated.
- Given only that /some/ coercion exists, is it runnable? *No.* Extracting
  $\co$ from $\exists \co.\ \judg{\varnothing}{\dc_1 \dcompat \dc_2 \coerce
  \co}$ needs choice, which yields a =noncomputable= constant.
- Does value finiteness help with that last one? *No* — it is proof
  irrelevance, not termination.

The last two points are the whole content of the =Prop= objection, and the fix
for them is to never erase: a derivation should be produced together with its
coercion, as a $\Sigma$-pair, the way an elaborating type checker returns a core
term alongside its typing derivation. It is not a reason to abandon indexing.

* 2 Option (c1): index the judgment by the coercion

Written out at the name level, with $\Gamma$ carrying coercions:

#+begin_src lean
inductive DescCompat (Σ : SymTab) : Assum → Name → Name → (Value → Value) → Prop
  | assum  {Γ a₁ a₂ c} :
      Γ.lookup (a₁, a₂) = some c →
      DescCompat Σ Γ a₁ a₂ c
  | unfold {Γ a₁ a₂ c} :
      (a₁, a₂) ∉ Γ →
      DescCompatD Σ (Γ.insert (a₁, a₂) c) (Σ a₁) (Σ a₂) c →
      DescCompat Σ Γ a₁ a₂ c
#+end_src

=unfold= does not *construct* $\mathsf{fix}\,x.\,\co$. It pre-substitutes: the
coercion is put into $\Gamma$ and the body is required to elaborate to that
same coercion. The premise is the unfolding equation, stated rather than
solved. This is legal (the recursive occurrence is strictly positive) and the
derivations are the finite ones, since $\Gamma$'s growth bound is untouched —
the keys are still name pairs and the function payload does not affect the
$\mathcal{O}(|\dom{\Sigma}|^2)$ argument of =sec:recursive-types=.

Note that this needs $\Gamma$ to map pairs to coercion *values*, which is
precisely the "alternative formulation" already floated in the second bullet of
=sec:judgments=. That alternative is not a stylistic variant; it is what makes
this rendering possible, because no rule then has to build a fixpoint.

** Where value finiteness earns its keep

The pre-substituted premise looks dangerously weak — any self-consistent $\co$
appears to satisfy it. It does not, and finiteness is the reason. Take the
=Tree=/=Tree'= example. The body derivation is a =D-Update=, whose conclusion
*forces* the shape of the lambda, so $\co$ must satisfy

\[ \co = \lambda \m.\ \{\, 1 \mapsto (\mathrm{dec}_{\mathtt{int64}} \circ
   \mathrm{enc}_{\mathtt{int32}})(\m[1]),\
   2 \mapsto \mathrm{map}\ \co\ (\m[2]) \,\}. \]

Because every value has a finite $\mdepth$, and the assumed coercion is only
ever applied under a message payload (the guardedness observation in
=sec:recursive-types=, and R4 step 1 of the October note), strong induction on
$\mdepth(\m)$ shows this equation has *exactly one* solution at every value. So
the derivation pins $\co$ down uniquely even though no rule ever built it. Were
values not finite, the premise would admit junk and the rule would be unsound.

In Lean this comes for free: =Value= is an inductive type, so finiteness is
structural rather than something to argue. The report has to work for it
(=sec:recursive-types=, point 2, with the 100-layer limit as backup); Lean is
handed it by the kernel.

This also gives the report's coherence remark a Lean reading. Uniqueness of the
fixpoint, not just agreement on encodable messages, is what makes "any two
derivations of the same judgment yield coercions that agree".

** What (c1) costs

$\co$ must be supplied up front. A derivation certifies a coercion; it does not
hand you one. So the "read rule by rule, it is a program" reading of
=sec:framework= becomes "read rule by rule, it is a specification a program can
be checked against". That is a change in the prose's framing, though not in any
statement, and option (c2) recovers most of it.

* 3 Option (c2): index the judgment by a recipe

Instead of a function, let the judgment output a first-order term in a small
language of coercions, with a denotation defined once.

#+begin_src lean
inductive Coe where
  | scalar (τ₁ τ₂ : ScalarType)                 -- T-Varint, T-StrByt, …
  | lift   (κ₁ κ₂ : Cardinality) (r : Coe)      -- the K-rules
  | upd    (a₂ : Name) (rs : List (Int × Coe))  -- D-Update
  | msgRef (a₁ a₂ : Name)                       -- N-Assum
#+end_src

with a finite recipe table $\Theta : (\mathrm{Name} \times \mathrm{Name})
\rightharpoonup \Coe$ produced alongside the derivation, and

\[ \interp{\cdot} : \Coe \to \mathrm{Value} \to \mathrm{Value}. \]

The judgment becomes $\judg{\Gamma}{\dc_1 \dcompat \dc_2 \coerce r}$ with
$r : \Coe$, and soundness becomes exactly the correctness statement the
=def:soundness= shape already wants:

\[ \judg{\varnothing}{\nm_1 \dcompat \nm_2 \coerce r} \implies
   \interp{r} \text{ is well behaved.} \]

** Why a recipe rather than a function: the checker becomes writable

This is the argument that decides it. =sec:recursive-types= gives two
*independent* finiteness facts. Under (c2) each one becomes the termination
measure of one Lean function, and the two functions are separate:

| Finiteness fact            | Lean function    | Measure            |
|----------------------------+------------------+--------------------|
| 1. $\Gamma$ grows, bounded | the checker      | $\Gamma$'s bound   |
| 2. every value is finite   | $\interp{\cdot}$ | writer $\mdepth$   |

The checker produces $(\Theta, r)$ and terminates on $\Gamma$'s
$\lvert\dom{\Sigma}\rvert^2$ bound; $\interp{\cdot}$ terminates on the writer
value's depth.

With a function-valued index this separation is unavailable. A checker
returning =Option (Value → Value)= would have to construct the function at the
=N-Unfold= step, which requires a fixpoint over values — but the checker has no
value argument, so the value recursion has to be built *inside* its return
value. The checker and the interpreter collapse into one tangled definition
carrying both measures at once. With a recipe the checker only ever assembles
first-order syntax; all value recursion lives in $\interp{\cdot}$, proved once
and reused by every witness.

That also answers the concern that drove this discussion. $\interp{\cdot}$ is a
single canonical function, but it is *parameterized by the recipe*, so distinct
derivations denote distinct coercions through it. That is categorically unlike
=Value.reinterpret=, which is a function of $(\dc_1, \dc_2)$ alone and leaves no
room for alternatives. Recipes give a whole space of runnable coercions at the
cost of one termination proof.

** Making guardedness syntactic

Note that $\Coe$ above has no $\mathsf{fix}$ and no variable. The recursion is
carried by the table $\Theta$, exactly as $\Sigma$ carries descriptor
recursion — *the recipe table is to the coercion what the symbol table is to
the descriptor.* =N-Unfold= writes an entry; =N-Assum= emits =msgRef a₁ a₂=,
whose interpretation is a table lookup.

This is worth the asymmetry with the report's $\mathsf{fix}\,x.\,\co$, because
it makes guardedness impossible to violate rather than merely true.
=msgRef= can only occur inside an =upd= at a key, and the interpreter's clause
for that key applies it to $\m[\id]$ — a strictly shallower value. So the
measure is a plain lexicographic pair: =scalar=/=lift=/=upd= shrink the recipe
at equal value, =msgRef= shrinks the value. Contrast the de Bruijn
$\mathsf{fix}$/$\mathsf{var}$ encoding, which is textually closer to the report
but where the environment holds functions whose arguments Lean cannot see are
smaller; there $\interp{\cdot}$ needs either fuel plus a stability lemma, or a
depth-indexed dependent environment. Both work and neither is pleasant.

So: =N-Unfold= as the report states it is *representable* either way, and the
table form is the one that keeps the interpreter two lines.

** What (c2) costs

- $\Coe$ has to track the rule set. Mitigated by the constructors tracking rule
  *families*: a new scalar axiom is a new $(\tau_1,\tau_2)$ pair, not a new
  constructor. Adding a rule shape is a new constructor plus a clause in
  $\interp{\cdot}$.
- $\interp{\cdot}$ is total, so it has a branch for a recipe that does not fit
  the value it meets (an =upd= arriving at a scalar). Making that branch
  $\init$ rather than anything arbitrary stops it being junk, and the soundness
  theorem only ever meets recipes that came from a derivation. The
  intrinsically typed alternative is worse here than it looks; see below.
- One unfolding layer in every statement: $\interp{r}$ where the report writes
  $\co$.
- The admissible coercions are now those $\Coe$ can express, not every
  well-behaved function. This is no loss against =def:compat=, which asks only
  that a well-behaved coercion *exist*, and the report already disclaims
  completeness.

** Indexing $\Coe$: why not intrinsically typed

This deserves more than a bullet, because "index $\Coe$ by the descriptor
pair" is two separate moves, and only one of them does what it is usually
invoked for.

*Indexing the recipe* is $\Coe : \mathrm{Desc} \to \mathrm{Desc} \to
\mathrm{Type}$, so =upd= can be built only at a pair where it makes sense.
*Making the denotation dependent* is $\denote{\cdot} : \mathrm{Desc} \to
\mathrm{Type}$, with $\interp{\cdot} : \Coe\ \dc_1\ \dc_2 \to \denote{\dc_1}
\to \denote{\dc_2}$. The off-spec branches of $\interp{\cdot}$ come from
ill-typed *values*, not ill-typed recipes — an =upd= meeting a scalar is a
fact about the =Value=, not about the recipe — so indexing the recipe alone
leaves them exactly where they were. Only the dependent denotation closes
them.

The dependent denotation is not available here. $\denote{\mathtt{Tree}} =
\mathbb{Z} \times \mathrm{Option}\ \denote{\mathtt{Tree}}$ has no solution as
a recursive function $\mathrm{Desc} \to \mathrm{Type}$, for the same reason
1.1 of the October note gives for =def:dsize= under a cyclic $\Sigma$. A real
type per descriptor would mean generating a fresh inductive per descriptor,
which Lean cannot do from data. So "intrinsically typed" can only mean the
subtype $\denote{\dc} \triangleq \{v : \mathrm{Value} \mid \mathrm{Valid}\
\dc\ v\}$, which does survive cycles: the carrier stays the one inductive
=Value= and only the predicate varies.

That subtype fuses a definition with its correctness theorem, and here that
carries a specific price. $\interp{\cdot}$ would have to *produce*
$\mathrm{Valid}\ \dc_2$ of its output, so =Value.reinterpret_valid= stops
being a theorem and becomes part of the definition — and =reinterpret_valid=
needs =Desc.OneofPreservedAll=, which =Proto/OneofCounterexample.lean= shows
is genuinely required in its recursive form rather than merely convenient. A
side condition the report states as the premise of one theorem would become a
permanent index on the coercion language, in a place the report never puts it.

Indexing also fights first-order-ness, and =upd= is where that becomes
unavoidable. Because =D-Update= quantifies over the shared key set, the
intrinsically typed constructor wants to be

#+begin_src lean
| upd : (∀ k, k ∈ shared d₁ d₂ → FieldCoe (d₁.get k) (d₂.get k)) → Coe d₁ d₂
#+end_src

a function-valued constructor argument. That costs exactly the properties
that made a recipe worth having: no =DecidableEq=, no structural recursion
over a recipe, nothing to print or diff, and a checker that must build a
dependent function instead of assembling syntax. Keeping it first-order as
=List (Int × Coe)= plus a proof that the list covers the shared keys at the
right types puts the typing back outside the term, merely relocated into the
constructor. Both at once is not on offer.

The implication most worth recording: *indexing slides (c2) back toward (a).*
An indexed $\Coe$ in =Type=, indexed by the descriptor pair, is a derivation
in =Type= with the pure side-condition premises erased — a smaller payload,
the same shape. The argument for the recipe above was that it is *not* a
derivation: first-order, decidable, and assemblable by a checker measured on
$\Gamma$'s growth. Index it and that argument weakens in proportion.

Two things intrinsic typing does buy, each with a cheaper answer.

- *A mis-paired recipe becomes a type error*, where raw =interp r v=
  typechecks for any =r=, so a mismatch is caught only by a theorem's
  hypotheses. Cheaper: $\judg{\Gamma}{\dc_1 \dcompat \dc_2 \coerce r}$ /is/
  the typing judgment for recipes, so the guarantee comes from a relation
  that is being defined anyway.
- *No off-spec branch*, so no theorem can accidentally be about it. Cheaper:
  make the branch $\init$. It is then the same default injection =D-Update=
  already prescribes for reader-only keys, and =Slot.reinterpret_isSome=
  (sketched in =sec:proto-transform=) is the lemma saying it never fires on a
  shared key.

Two smaller points against. A structural $\denote{\dc}$ built as a product
over the entry list would put the sealed representation into a *type*, which
=lean/CLAUDE.md= bars even from statements; the subtype form avoids it, since
=Value.Valid= is sanctioned. And this development has already made the same
call three times — the =Serializer ι α wf= phantom predicate, InterParse's
relations carrying no well-formedness premises with the callers holding them,
and =Value.Valid= as a predicate rather than a subtype. An intrinsic $\Coe$
would be the first break in a deliberate convention.

*Recommendation:* raw $\Coe$, $\init$ as the off-spec branch, and the
compatibility relation as its typing judgment.

* 4 Where this leaves =Value.reinterpret=

Not wasted, and no longer load-bearing. Under (c1) or (c2) it becomes *a*
witness rather than the definition: prove $\interp{r_0} = $ =Value.reinterpret
d₁ d₂= for the canonical recipe $r_0$ if its existing lemma set is wanted, and
skip the bridge otherwise. Nothing else depends on the equation.

The =% TODO= at the end of =sec:log-rel-lean= should be read in this light. The
termination metric does move to the writer's value's depth, but it moves into
$\interp{\cdot}$. That switch is not an optimization: as 1.1 of the October note
shows, =descSize= has *no* solution for a cyclic $\Sigma$, so a descriptor
measure cannot survive the move to a symbol table at all. The reader's field
list is still consulted for totality over $\dc_2$, but one layer at a time
through =explode=, which is a fold over a finite key set rather than a
recursion.

* 6 Suggested report text

For =sec:log-rel-lean=, replacing the sentence before the itemize and adding a
third item:

#+begin_src latex
The paper formalization reads a derivation of
$\judg{\varnothing}{\dc_1 \dcompat \dc_2 \coerce \co}$ as the construction of
$\co$. Lean cannot eliminate a \texttt{Prop} into a function, so a derivation
in \texttt{Prop} cannot be \emph{computed from}. It can, however, be
\emph{indexed by} the coercion: a \texttt{Prop}-valued family may take
arguments of any sort, and reading the coercion off a derivation is then a
projection from its type rather than an elimination. Three alternatives follow.
\begin{itemize}
\item Put the derivation into \texttt{Type} \ldots
\item Keep the existing \texttt{Value.reinterpret} \ldots
\item \textbf{Make the coercion an output of the judgment.} Index the relation
  by the coercion it builds, so that the Lean statement is literally
  $\judg{\Gamma}{\dc_1 \dcompat \dc_2 \coerce \co}$, and state soundness as
  ``the coercion a derivation carries is well-behaved''
  (Definition~\ref{def:well-behaved}). Nothing is eliminated and no second
  function is defined, so neither the duplication of the first alternative nor
  the agreement lemma of the second arises. Two facts make this work. The
  recursive rule states the unfolding equation rather than solving it, and
  because every message has finite depth that equation has a unique solution,
  so a derivation still determines its coercion. Separately, the coercion may
  be carried as a first-order \emph{recipe} with a denotation defined once;
  then $\mathsf{fix}$ is a constructor rather than a fixed point, and the two
  finiteness facts of Section~\ref{sec:recursive-types} become the termination
  measures of two separate functions --- the assumption set's growth bound for
  the checker, the writer value's depth for the denotation.
\end{itemize}
#+end_src

And, if the recipe variant is adopted, for =sec:judgments=:

#+begin_src latex
\begin{figure}
\[
\begin{array}{lcl}
  r &::=& \mathrm{sc}(\ty_1, \ty_2) \mid \mathrm{lift}(\card_1, \card_2, r)
        \mid \mathrm{upd}(\nm_2, \overline{\id \mapsto r}) \\
    &&\mid \mathrm{ref}(\nm_1, \nm_2) \\
  \Theta &::=& \overline{(\nm_1, \nm_2) \mapsto r}
\end{array}
\]
\caption{Coercion recipes. $\Theta$ carries recursion for coercions exactly as
  $\Sigma$ carries it for descriptors; $\mathrm{ref}$ resolves through it.}
\end{figure}

A judgment $\judg{\Gamma}{\dc_1 \dcompat \dc_2 \coerce r}$ outputs a recipe,
and $\llbracket r \rrbracket_{\Theta} : \denote{\dc_1} \rightarrow
\denote{\dc_2}$ is its denotation. $\mathrm{ref}$ occurs only inside an
$\mathrm{upd}$ at a message
field, so $\llbracket \cdot \rrbracket_{\Theta}$ applies it only to a nested
message of strictly smaller depth; this is the guardedness condition
Section~\ref{sec:recursive-types} describes, here imposed by the syntax.
#+end_src
