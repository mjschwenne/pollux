● This is a strong section conceptually — and the most interesting thing I can
  tell you is that it and proto-design.org have independently arrived at
  complementary halves of the same standard construction. Here's my assessment.

  The verdict up front

  The section is less a new direction than a name for what the formalization
  already does. compatInterParseOk is literally the statement "the syntactic
  judgment d₁ ⋘ d₂ implies the semantic one, with f = compatTransform" — the
  existential ∃f in your semantic definition is never opaque in practice; the
  codebase always exhibits the canonical witness (compatTransform in InterParse,
  Value.reinterpret in Proto). Your closing line — "this is basically a logical
  relation in disguise" — is exactly right, and it's true of the existing
  artifact, not just the proposal: syntactic relation = typing judgment,
  semantic definition = the logical relation, the round-trip theorem = the
  fundamental lemma. The section's genuinely new content is the claim that when
  recursion arrives, you keep the semantic definition primary and demote the
  relation to a sound decision procedure. That is feasible, it's the right call
  in Lean specifically, and it converges with what proto-design.org already
  decided ("restate as pointwise one-layer compatibility over the finite name
  graph") — the org file has the checker half, the tex section has the
  justification half.

  Making the Löb rule precise

  The rule μF ≪ μG ⊢ F(μF) ≪ G(μG) is, made precise, Amadio–Cardelli-style
  subtyping of equi-recursive types (also Brandt–Henglein's cyclic
  axiomatization; TAPL ch. 21 is the accessible presentation). With the planned
  flat symbol table, descriptors are regular trees, so the judgment becomes Σ ⊢ 
  d₁ ≪ d₂ where Σ is a finite set of assumed name pairs. Derivations are finite
  for free: Σ grows monotonically inside the finite pair set, and your survey
  says the graphs are tiny (533 cycles, max SCC 22). This is a plain inductive
  relation — no coinduction anywhere.

  Soundness of the checker against the semantic definition is then a strong
  induction on message size, and this is where your Stream/List observation
  becomes the load-bearing fact rather than an aside: protobuf values are finite
  even under cyclic schemas, so when the proof re-encounters a pair assumed in
  Σ, it has necessarily descended under a .msg payload and the value has
  strictly shrunk. This is Appel–McAllester step-indexing where the index is
  just the value's size — an ordinary Nat, no transfinite machinery. So
  coinduction is avoided twice over, by two independent finiteness facts:
  syntactically because regular trees have finitely many subterm pairs,
  semantically because messages are finite. Either alone would suffice; you have
  both. The section should say this explicitly.

  There's also a correspondence worth putting in the report because it's
  genuinely pretty: in the rule ⊨ μX.f : μF ≼ μG, the context variable is
  literally the recursive call of reinterpret in its own definition. Look at
  Payload.reinterpret's .msg arm in Transform.lean:132 — after the symbol-table
  refactor that recursive call is exactly the assumed transform in the context,
  and guardedness of the fixpoint (the call sits under a .msg payload) is
  exactly why value-size induction discharges the assumption. The typing rule as
  written in the section mixes things up (it binds X but types f, and puts ⊨ in
  the conclusion of a ⊢ rule); the corrected shape is: from x : d₁ ≼ d₂ ⊢ body 
  : F(d₁) ≼ G(d₂) conclude ⊨ fix x. body : μF ≼ μG.

  Feasibility in Lean, concretely

  The motivation is stronger in Lean than it would be in Rocq: Lean has no
  native coinductive predicates and no paco. You'd be hand-rolling a gfp over a
  mutual four-relation block, with hand-written bisimulation principles — and
  CLAUDE.md already documents how painful the mutual inductive eliminators are
  (the 23-?_ recursor dance). Avoiding coinduction here isn't an aesthetic
  preference; it's dodging the worst tooling gap in the ecosystem.

  What actually changes when the symbol table lands, under this plan:

  - The compat relation gains the Σ context (or equivalently becomes a finite
  fixpoint over the reachable-pair graph). Since Proto's compat relations don't
  exist yet, you can design their soundness statement against the semantic
  definition from day one — then recursion later touches only the checker, not
  the theorem statements.
  - reinterpret's termination measure moves from descSize d₂ to the writer's
  value size. Mechanical: the recursion only enters .msg payloads, which are
  structurally smaller. Notably Field.init never recurses into message
  descriptors (explicit presence ⇒ optional none), so the totality machinery is
  untouched by cyclic schemas.
  - Value.Valid already works unchanged — it's value-structural with the
  descriptor consulted by get?, a design made for other reasons that happens to
  be exactly what cyclic descriptors need.
  - Desc.OneofPreservedAll (currently WF-recursion on descSize d₂) becomes a
  finite conjunction over reachable pairs — the same memoized-pair pattern as
  the relation itself.

  What I would not do: literally formalize the μ-type presentation in Lean —
  descriptors as functors, interpretation into Lean types. That's a
  data-generic-programming research project (positivity and universe issues,
  opaque interpretations disconnected from the wire-format proofs) for zero
  proof leverage. Keep the algebraic view as the report's explanatory lens; the
  Lean realizations of its pieces are: μ = the symbol table, unfolding =
  explode's one resolution step, the Löb rule = the Σ-context, the semantic
  judgment = the planned Encodes-based statement.

  Sharpenings the section needs

  - The functor equation is off. F X = int + int × F X mentions F X on the right
  — it should be F X = int + int × X with the type being μX. F X. And with
  explicit presence, the honest shape is μX. option int × option X, not a sum:
  the sum form suggests a nil/cons choice that protobuf fields don't have. The
  list-ness comes from finiteness of encodings, which is actually a cleaner
  version of your point.
  - ∃bs should become ∀bs. As written, the definition is the weak leg — some
  encoding of m parses to f(m), i.e., the functional serializer's round trip.
  The property schema evolution actually promises is ∀bs, Encodes_{d₁}(m, bs) → 
  parse_{d₂}(bs) = f(m) — quantifying over other people's serializers, which is
  the whole point, and it's exactly the parser-completeness leg proto-design.org
  already commits to. (Also Encodes_{d₁}(m,bs) = bs is a typo — the relation
  just holds.)
  - The framing invites a transitivity question, and the answer is a highlight.
  Once the drop rule exists (it does, in Value.reinterpret), evolution does not
  compose: d₁ drops field 5 to get d₂, d₃ re-adds field 5 at a different type —
  adjacent pairs compatible, composite incompatible. The type-system view
  explains why protobuf has the reserved keyword: it's the side condition that
  restores composability. This would make a great counterexample file in the
  OneofCounterexample.lean style, and a great report paragraph — the framework
  earning its keep by explaining a language feature.

  Understandability

  As report prose it currently reads as exploratory notes — the thesis ("this is
  a logical relation") arrives in the last sentence, and the strongest
  technical observation (Stream is really List, hence induction suffices) is
  presented as a parenthetical. I'd invert it: lead with the logical-relations
  claim, make finiteness-of-values the central argument, and anchor to the
  literature (Amadio–Cardelli, Brandt–Henglein, Appel–McAllester) so a reader
  recognizes the Löb rule as the standard recursive-subtyping rule rather than a
  conjecture — that also lets you delete the "likely not actually correct"
  hedge and state the corrected rule. There are surface typos to catch ("would
  would", "were we see", "prospective", "we even if we"). And I'd add one
  paragraph connecting to the survey data: the checker this yields is not just
  sound but cheap, because real SCCs are tiny — that's the bridge between this
  section and the corpus section that makes the design feel inevitable rather
  than speculative.
