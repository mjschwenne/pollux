* Summary

=sec:philosophy= says that parsing can be split into two steps:

1. Read the bytes into a plain, low-level form, without looking at a
   descriptor.
2. Use the descriptor to turn that low-level form into the final message.

The Lean doesn't do this today. Every parser in it reads and interprets in
one pass.

The TODO after =def:sem-compat-fld= asks whether the report should introduce a
new $\assemble$ notation, or instead restrict the byte string to "the fragments
that matter to this slot". This note argues that these are the same answer,
and that the answer is the two steps of =sec:philosophy=:

- *Step one* turns bytes into a list of *records*. A record is one tag plus
  its raw payload. It needs no descriptor.
- *"The fragments that matter to this slot"* are simply the records with that
  slot's field number.
- *$\assemble$* is step two for a single field: it builds the reader's slot
  from that field's records.

Seen this way, the philosophy section and the type-theory section describe the
same thing, and the two-step split does real work in the proofs. It is what
lets message-level compatibility (=def:sem-compat=) be proven one field at a
time from field-level compatibility (=def:sem-compat-fld=). The note also
argues that the split should be *optional* in the general framework, because
some formats, including Avro and the report's own InterParse format, have no
step one.

* The two steps for protobuf

** Step one: bytes to records

A protobuf message on the wire is a sequence of *records*. Each record is a tag
followed by a payload. The tag holds two things: the field number $k$, and the
*wire type* $w$, which says how long the payload is. The payload's content
depends on the wire type:

| Wire type | What the record holds               |
|-----------+-------------------------------------|
| =VARINT=    | an unsigned number, up to $2^{64} - 1$ |
| =I64=       | exactly 8 bytes                     |
| =I32=       | exactly 4 bytes                     |
| =LEN=       | a length, then that many bytes      |

(The deprecated group wire types =SGROUP= and =EGROUP= are left out, as the
report's descriptors leave out groups.)

Because the wire type always says where the payload ends, the whole byte
string can be cut into records without knowing anything about the message.
Write that function as
\[ \records : \mathrm{Bytes} \to \mathrm{Option}\ (\mathrm{List}\ (k, w, p)). \]
It fails only on malformed input: a truncated payload, an unknown wire type, or
field number 0. The list keeps the records in the order they appear.

The important choice is to *keep every =LEN= payload as raw bytes*. A =LEN=
payload could be a string, raw bytes, a packed list of numbers, or a nested
message, and the bytes alone can't say which. =sec:philosophy= describes how
=protoscope= copes with this by guessing (its =-all-fields-are-messages=
option), and how the guess can be wrong. Step one doesn't need to guess. It
leaves the question for step two, where the descriptor answers it.

One piece of notation: for a set of field numbers $K$, $\restr{rs}{K}$ means
"the records of $rs$ whose field number is in $K$, in their original order".
For one field, $\restr{rs}{k}$ is short for $\restr{rs}{\{k\}}$.

** Step two: records to a message

Step two looks at the reader's descriptor $d$ and builds the message one field
at a time.

*Which records belong to a field.* A field accepts a record when the record's
wire type fits the field's type. For example, an =int32= field accepts
=VARINT=, and a =string= field accepts =LEN=. A *repeated* field of a type that
can be packed (numbers and =bool=) accepts both its own wire type and =LEN=,
because protobuf requires readers to take a repeated number field in either
packed or unpacked form. Write this as $\accepts(f, w)$. A record that no field
accepts is *skipped*. Either its field number isn't in $d$, or its wire type
doesn't fit the field with that number.

*Reading one record.* Each accepted record gives one or more values of the
field's type $\tau$:
- a =VARINT= record gives one number, converted to $\tau$. For example, an
  =int32= field takes the low 32 bits as a signed number, and a =sint32= field
  undoes zig-zag. This is where the conversions of =T-Varint= happen;
- an =I32= or =I64= record gives one value from its 4 or 8 bytes;
- a =LEN= record for a =string= or =bytes= field gives one value;
- a =LEN= record for a packed repeated field gives a list of values;
- a =LEN= record for a message field gives one nested message. To get it, the
  reader runs both steps again on the payload bytes, using the nested
  descriptor. This is the parse/validate loop drawn in =fig:parse-validate=.

*Building one field's slot.* Given the accepted records $rs$ for a field
$f = \langle \kappa, \tau \rangle$, the reader's slot is $\assemble_f(rs)$:
\begin{align*}
  \assemble_{\langle \imp, \tau \rangle}(rs) &=
    \begin{cases}
      \imp\ \init_\tau & rs = [\,] \\
      \imp\ (v_1 \comb{\tau} \cdots \comb{\tau} v_n) & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases} \\
  \assemble_{\langle \opt, \tau \rangle}(rs) &=
    \begin{cases}
      \opt\ \none & rs = [\,] \\
      \opt\ (\some{(v_1 \comb{\tau} \cdots \comb{\tau} v_n)}) & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases} \\
  \assemble_{\langle \rep, \tau \rangle}(rs) &= \rep\ (\text{all values of all records of } rs, \text{ in order})
\end{align*}
Here $v_1, \dots, v_n$ are the values read from the records of $rs$, in order.
$\comb{\tau}$ is the report's combine operator: for scalars the later value
wins, and for messages the two are merged. $\assemble$ fails if reading any
record fails, for example on a nested message that doesn't parse.

*Oneof groups.* Fields in the same oneof can't be built independently. When a
record for one member arrives, protobuf clears the other members. So the reader
treats a whole oneof group as one unit. It finds the member $k^*$ of the last
record in the group, gives every other member $\opt\ \none$, and builds $k^*$'s
slot from the final unbroken run of $k^*$'s records. (Records for $k^*$ that
came before a different member were cleared.) Every field that isn't in a oneof
is a unit on its own. Write $\groups(d)$ for these units: each oneof's field
numbers form one group, and every other field number is a group of one.

*The whole parser.*
\[
  \parse_d(bs) = \some{(m, U)} \quad\text{where}\quad
  \begin{aligned}[t]
    rs &= \records(bs), \\
    m &= \text{for each group } B \in \groups(d), \text{ the slots built from } \restr{rs}{B}, \\
    U &= \text{the field numbers of the records of } rs \text{ that were skipped}.
  \end{aligned}
\]

** The property that matters: each group reads only its own records

Because $\parse$ is built this way, *the slots of group $B$ depend only on
$\restr{\records(bs)}{B}$*. Records for other fields, and the order of records
across different fields, can't affect them. This is what the TODO's
"restricting the byte string to the fragments that matter to this slot" means,
made precise. Bytes can't be restricted directly; records can.

* Answering the TODO after def:sem-compat-fld

** Defining Encodes record by record

=def:sem-compat-fld= uses $\Encodes_f^k(s, rs)$, "the records an encoding may
contain for key $k$ when the slot holds $s$". With step one, the
message-level $\Encodes$ can be *defined* from this field-level one, instead of
the field-level one being described in words after the fact:
\[
  \Encodes_d(m, bs) \iff
  \records(bs) = \some{rs}
  \ \wedge\ \text{every record of } rs \text{ has a field number in } \dom{d}
  \ \wedge\ \forall k \in \dom{d}.\ \Encodes_{d[k]}^k(m[k],\ \restr{rs}{k}).
\]
In words: cut the bytes into records. Then each field's own records, read in
order, must encode that field's value. Nothing constrains how records of
*different* fields are ordered relative to each other, so this automatically
allows fields to be written in any order, which protobuf permits.

The field level, $\Encodes_f^k(s, rs)$, says that every record of $rs$ has
field number $k$ and:
- for $s = \imp\ p$: either $rs$ is empty and $p = \init_\tau$, or $rs$ is
  non-empty, each record holds a valid encoding of a value of $\tau$, and
  combining those values with $\comb{\tau}$ gives $p$;
- for $s = \opt\ \none$: $rs$ is empty;
- for $s = \opt\ (\some{p})$: as the non-empty case of $\imp$;
- for $s = \rep\ ps$: putting together the values of all the records, in
  order, gives $ps$. For a packable type each record may be packed or unpacked,
  and the two forms may be mixed.

A nested message $v$ inside a =LEN= record is encoded when
$\Encodes_{d'}(v, p)$ holds for the payload bytes $p$, so the definition refers
to itself one level down. This matches the report's list of what an encoding
may do (=sec:type-theory=, the list after the cardinality rules): it may write
an implicit field that holds its default, repeat a singular field's record,
split a singular message across several records, and pick packed or unpacked
form.

** A revised def:sem-compat-fld, with no new notation

$\assemble$ is no longer an invented notation. It is the piece of $\parse$
that handles one field. The field-level definition becomes:
\[
  \vDash_n g : f_1 \propto f_2 \iff
  \forall s \checkmark^{f_1},\ rs.\
  \mathrm{depth}(s) \le n \rightarrow
  \Encodes^k_{f_1}(s, rs) \rightarrow
  \bigl(\forall (k, w, p) \in rs.\ \accepts(f_2, w)\bigr)
  \ \wedge\ \assemble_{f_2}(rs) = \some{(g\ s)}.
\]
The first conjunct is "no record of $rs$ is skipped", stated exactly. It is the
field-level form of the faithfulness condition in =def:well-behaved=.

** The message-level statement follows from the field-level one

This is the payoff. The per-group property above turns the =D-Update= case of
the soundness proof into a field-by-field argument.

*Lemma (message from fields).* Suppose
- $\mathrm{OneofPreserved}(d_1, d_2)$,
- for every shared field number $k \in \dom{d_1} \cap \dom{d_2}$,
  $\vDash_n g_k : d_1[k] \propto d_2[k]$, and
- $f$ is the coercion =D-Update= builds from the $g_k$: it applies $g_k$ at
  shared keys and $\init(d_2[k])$ at keys only $d_2$ has.

Then for every $m \checkmark^{d_1}$ with $\mathrm{depth}_v(m) \le n$ and every
$bs$ with $\Encodes_{d_1}(m, bs)$,
\[ \parse_{d_2}(bs) = \some{(f(m), U)} \quad\text{with}\quad U \subseteq \dom{d_1} \setminus \dom{d_2}. \]

*Why it holds.* Let $rs = \records(bs)$, and look at each field number $k$ of
$d_2$:
- *$k$ is shared.* $\restr{rs}{k}$ encodes $m[k]$ under $d_1[k]$, by the
  definition of $\Encodes_{d_1}$. The field-level assumption says every one of
  these records is accepted, and $\assemble_{d_2[k]}$ gives $g_k(m[k])$.
- *Only $d_2$ has $k$.* The writer only writes records for its own fields, so
  $\restr{rs}{k}$ is empty, and $\assemble$ gives $\init(d_2[k])$.
- *Only $d_1$ has $k$.* These records match no field of $d_2$, so they're
  skipped and their field number goes into $U$. They are the only skipped
  records, because the first case showed that every record of a shared field is
  accepted. So $U \subseteq \dom{d_1} \setminus \dom{d_2}$.

Oneof groups need one more step, and it is exactly what $\mathrm{OneofPreserved}$
provides. Suppose $d_2$ puts two shared fields in one oneof group.
$\mathrm{OneofPreserved}$ says $d_1$ had them in one group too. A valid writer
message sets at most one member of each of its groups, so at most one member of
the reader's group has records. The group step then reduces to the single-field
step for that member. Every other member gets $\opt\ \none$, which is also what
the coercion gives, since =K-Opt= maps $\none$ to $\none$. Without
$\mathrm{OneofPreserved}$, the reader could see records for two members and
clear one of them, and the lemma would be false. The Lean comment on
=Desc.OneofPreserved= (=Transform.lean:341=) predicts this.

This lemma also gives =def:soundness= its link to semantic compatibility, which
is what the =% TODO= after =def:soundness= asks for. The lemma's conclusion is
=def:sem-compat= (the equation) together with the faithfulness half of
=def:well-behaved= (the bound on $U$). So the outer induction on depth, applied
to it, proves both at once.

** A small wording issue in def:well-behaved

The text after =def:well-behaved= describes $U$ as "the set of fields skipped
during parsing (i.e. where defaults are injected during parsing)". These are
two different sets. Records are skipped at fields only the writer has.
Defaults are injected at fields only the reader has, which have no records at
all, so nothing there is skipped. The condition
$U \subseteq \dom{d_1} \setminus \dom{d_2}$ is about the first set. Suggested
wording: "$U$ is the set of field numbers whose records the reader skipped,
because the field number is unknown to it or the wire type doesn't fit."

* What this means for sec:philosophy

** How the section lines up with the two steps

| sec:philosophy says                                 | In the two-step reading                                                                                             |
|-----------------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =VARINT= fields are unsigned numbers, converted later | Correct. Step one stores the unsigned number; reading a record converts it to the field's type                      |
| =I32= / =I64= are only bytes until validation           | Correct. Step one stores 4 or 8 bytes                                                                               |
| =LEN= is the tricky case; protoscope has to guess     | No guess needed. Step one keeps the bytes; step two knows what they are                                             |
| Parse and validate loop for nested messages         | Reading a message-typed =LEN= record runs both steps again on its payload                                             |
| The compatibility relations never mention the bytes | Correct for the relations. The semantic definitions mention records, and only $\records$ and $\Encodes$ touch bytes |

The last row is the honest version of the section's closing claim. "The work
lifts trivially to the protoscope level" undersells it. The field-level
definitions in =sec:type-theory= are *already* stated at the protoscope level,
because records are what protoscope shows.

One caution about the word "validation". For protobuf, step two does much more
than check. It converts numbers between types, combines repeated records,
decides what each =LEN= payload is, and parses nested messages. All of protobuf's
interesting compatibility behaviour happens there: =int32= vs. =int64= on
negative numbers, =sint32= vs. =int32=, =bool= reading a wider number,
=string= vs. =bytes=, packed vs. unpacked. Calling step two "decoding" or
"assembly" would set the right expectation. Calling it "validation" fits CBOR
with CDDL, where step two really only checks.

* Other formats

The point of keeping the split is that Pollux should apply to formats other
than protobuf. Here is how a few formats fit:

| Format              | Is there a step one?                                                                  | What step two does                         |
|---------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------|
| Protobuf            | Yes: records                                                                          | converts, combines, parses nested messages |
| CBOR + CDDL         | Yes: any well-formed CBOR item decodes on its own                                     | checks the item against the CDDL rule      |
| Avro                | No: the bytes have no tags or lengths; the writer's schema is needed to read anything | (no split)                                 |
| InterParse (report) | No: a tag has no wire type, so an unknown field can't be skipped                      | (no split)                                 |

InterParse is worth pointing out. Its reader can't skip a field it doesn't know,
because the tag doesn't say how long the payload is. That is why its
compatibility relation has no rule for dropping fields, and why its proofs work
on whole messages (=compatInterParseOk=), not one field at a time.

So the recommendation is in two layers:

1. *The part every format provides.* Descriptors, values that are valid for a
   descriptor, the compatibility relation with its coercion, and one
   format-specific promise: the message-level statement of =def:sem-compat=
   (every encoding under $d_1$ parses under $d_2$ to the coerced message). This
   is the role =def:compat= used to play. I'd restore it in this form, as the
   format-independent definition, rather than drop it.
2. *An extra the format can offer.* When a format has a step one (a
   descriptor-free $\records$) and a step two built group by group, the
   message-level statement follows from field-level ones, as in the lemma
   above. Protobuf and CBOR offer it. Avro and InterParse don't, and they still
   fit layer 1.

* Lean

*Today.* Nothing in the Lean follows the two steps.
- InterParse's =parseVal= (=InterParse/Parser.lean:62=) reads a tag, looks it
  up in the descriptor, and only then knows how to read the payload. Its
  =parseValue'= does have a small second step: =listToValue= combines the
  parsed =(key, Val)= list according to the descriptor.
- The Proto layer has no parser yet, and its =Value= is the final form of the
  message, not records.
- =Value.reinterpret= is the =D-Update= coercion, so the lemma above is about
  it once a Proto parser exists.

*Once the report settles this*, the Proto parser can be built to match.
Naming and file layout are Lean-side choices; the definitions themselves wait
on the report.
- A record type and =records : List UInt8 → Option (List Record)=.
- A per-field =assemble=, handling oneof groups as one unit.
- =parse d= defined as the composition of the two. Then "each group reads only
  its own records" is true by definition rather than a lemma to prove.
- =Encodes= defined record by record, as above.

Two side benefits:
- *Termination is easy.* Step one only consumes bytes, and every nested parse
  runs on a =LEN= payload strictly shorter than the bytes it came from. So the
  parser terminates by byte length, whatever the descriptor looks like. That
  holds for recursive descriptors too (=sec:recursive-types=).
- *Unknown fields come for free.* The skipped records are already at hand.
  Returning their field numbers gives $U$. Returning the records themselves
  would model protobuf's unknown-field preservation, which is question F2 in
  =2026-09-30-review-coercion-framing.org=.

Nothing in the Lean is blocked by this note.

* Questions for the author

1. *How generous should $\Encodes$ be?* The definition above allows everything
   real parsers accept: repeated records for a singular field (with arbitrary
   earlier values), messages split across records, and packed and unpacked
   records mixed. The soundness theorem quantifies over every encoding, so a
   more generous $\Encodes$ gives a stronger theorem, and the cardinality rules
   that are already marked unsound (=K-ImpOpt=, =K-OptRep=, …) are exactly
   those this generosity breaks. Two smaller cases aren't settled: number
   encodings padded with extra bytes, and empty packed records.
2. *Should $U$ hold field numbers or whole records?* Field numbers are enough
   for faithfulness. Records are needed if Pollux ever models unknown-field
   preservation.
3. *Restore =def:compat=?* In the format-independent form described under
   "Other formats", or point =sec:inter-parse= at =def:sem-compat= instead.
4. *Name for step two.* "Validation" fits CBOR but undersells protobuf.
   "Assembly" matches the existing $\assemble$.

% sec:philosophy, replacing the LEN bullet
\item \texttt{LEN} is the tricky case: its bytes could be a string, raw bytes,
  a packed list of numbers, or a nested message. \texttt{protoscope} guesses
  (\texttt{-all-fields-are-messages}) and can guess wrong. Pollux does not
  guess. The first step keeps every \texttt{LEN} payload as raw bytes, and the
  second step, which has the descriptor, decides what they are. For a nested
  message this means running both steps again on the payload, which is the loop
  in Figure~\ref{fig:parse-validate}.

% sec:philosophy, replacing the closing paragraph
For Protobuf, the first step cuts the bytes into \emph{records}: a field
number, a wire type, and a raw payload. The wire type says where each payload
ends, so this needs no descriptor:
\[ \records : \mathrm{Bytes} \to \mathrm{Option}\ (\mathrm{List}\ (k, w, p)). \]
The second step builds the reader's message one field at a time. The slot for
field $k$ is $\assemble_{d[k]}$ applied to $\restr{\records(bs)}{k}$, the
records with field number $k$. A oneof group is handled as one unit, since a
record for one member clears the others. Records whose field number is unknown
to the reader, or whose wire type doesn't fit, are skipped. For Protobuf this
second step does more than validate: it converts numbers between types,
combines repeated records, and parses nested messages, and that is where all of
Protobuf's compatibility behaviour lives.

The compatibility relations never mention bytes. The semantic definitions of
Section~\ref{sec:type-theory} mention records, and only $\records$ and
$\mathrm{Encodes}$ mention bytes. Because each field's slot depends only on
that field's records, compatibility of whole messages follows from
compatibility of their fields.

Not every format has a first step. Avro needs the writer's schema to read
anything, and the format of Section~\ref{sec:inter-parse} cannot skip an
unknown field because its tags carry no wire type. Pollux's definition of
compatibility (Definition~\ref{def:compat}) does not depend on the split. The
split is an extra that makes field-by-field proofs possible when a format
offers it.
#+end_src

For =sec:type-theory=, replacing the TODO and the paragraph that defines
$\Encodes_f^k$ and $\assemble$:

#+begin_src latex
Here $\mathrm{Encodes}_f^k(s, rs)$ says that $rs$, a list of records with field
number $k$, is a valid encoding of slot $s$. The message-level relation is
defined from it:
\[
  \mathrm{Encodes}_d(m, bs) \iff
  \records(bs) = \some{rs}
  \wedge \forall (k, w, p) \in rs.\ k \in \dom{d}
  \wedge \forall k \in \dom{d}.\ \mathrm{Encodes}_{d[k]}^k(m[k], \restr{rs}{k}).
\]
Records of different fields may appear in any order. $\assemble_f(rs)$ is the
part of $\mathrm{parse}$ that builds one slot from that field's records
(Section~\ref{sec:philosophy}):
\begin{itemize}
\item An empty list gives $\init_\tau$, $\none$, or $[\,]$.
\item Singular slots combine the records' values with $\comb{\tau}$.
\item Repeated slots concatenate the records' values, packed or unpacked.
\end{itemize}
"No record of $rs$ is skipped" means
$\forall (k, w, p) \in rs.\ \accepts(f_2, w)$.

\begin{lemma}[Message from fields]\label{lem:msg-from-fields}
  Suppose $\mathrm{OneofPreserved}(d_1, d_2)$, and
  $\vDash_n g_k : d_1[k] \propto d_2[k]$ for every
  $k \in \dom{d_1} \cap \dom{d_2}$. Let $f$ be the coercion
  \textsc{D-Update} builds from the $g_k$. Then for all
  $m \checkmark^{d_1}$ with $\mathrm{depth}_v(m) \le n$ and all $bs$ with
  $\mathrm{Encodes}_{d_1}(m, bs)$,
  \[ \mathrm{parse}_{d_2}(bs) = \some{(f(m), U)} \quad\text{with}\quad
     U \subseteq \dom{d_1} \setminus \dom{d_2}. \]
\end{lemma}
#+end_src
